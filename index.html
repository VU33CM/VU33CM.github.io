What if your voice could cross the world?

Welcome to Amateur Radio — a world where a microphone, a radio and a piece of wire can become a doorway to people, places, satellites and signals far beyond your horizon.

Start here

You don't need to know radio.
Just follow the signal.

Let's begin with something simple: you speak into a microphone. Somewhere between your voice and another person's ears, something remarkable happens.

It begins with information

Your voice becomes a signal.

A radio doesn't send your voice through the air exactly as you speak it. The transmitter processes that information and places it onto a radio-frequency signal. That signal is ready to travel.

The radio station

Then comes the invisible part.

The transmitter creates energy at a chosen radio frequency. The antenna couples that energy into the surrounding electromagnetic field. You cannot see the wave — but you can measure it, receive it and use it to carry information.

That is the heart of amateur radio: learning how to create, control, receive and experiment with radio signals.

The antenna

The antenna is your doorway into the electromagnetic world.

Different frequencies behave differently, and different antennas are designed to work with them. A wire, vertical, dipole, Yagi or beam can turn the electrical energy in your station into electromagnetic energy — and do the reverse when receiving.

Suddenly, a piece of metal on a roof or mast is no longer just an antenna. It is part of the conversation.

The sky changes everything

And suddenly, distance is no longer simple.

A VHF signal might reach a nearby repeater. An HF signal can travel great distances under the right propagation conditions. A satellite can become a moving relay overhead. And with Moonbounce, radio amateurs have even used the Moon as a reflector.

The same hobby can take you from your neighbourhood to another continent — or beyond the atmosphere.

And this is where it becomes yours

Build. Listen. Experiment. Contact the world.

You can study how radio works, build an antenna, listen for distant signals, learn a digital mode, call a repeater, work a satellite, exchange QSL cards, join a net, chase DX or simply discover why a signal reached somewhere you never expected.

There is no single way to enjoy amateur radio. The radio becomes whatever you are curious enough to explore.
