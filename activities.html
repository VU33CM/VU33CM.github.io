FIRST, THE GATEWAY

Amateur radio begins with a licence.

Before transmitting, an operator needs the appropriate authorization. That licence opens a surprisingly large world of communication, experimentation and learning.

The licence is your gateway.

Once licensed, the radio spectrum becomes a place to explore responsibly. You can communicate, experiment with equipment, learn about propagation and participate in amateur-radio activities.

FOUR BIG WORLDS

There isn't one way to do amateur radio.

The same station can take you into voice communication, digital modes, computer networks, portable operation, propagation experiments and space communication.

🎙️

Voice & RF

Talk locally, across a country or around the world using different bands and antennas.

💻

Digital Radio

Use computers and digital protocols to exchange text, data, images and weak signals.

🌐

Hybrid Radio

Combine radio with internet-linked systems such as EchoLink, AllStar and IRLP.

🛰️

Space & Propagation

Use satellites, the ISS, unusual propagation and even the Moon as part of the radio path.

01 • HF RADIO

The first great adventure is distance.

High-frequency radio can turn a modest station into a window onto the world. The ionosphere becomes part of the communication system.

HF • DX • WORLDWIDE

Talk across continents.

HF operators use voice, Morse and digital modes to make contacts over enormous distances.

  • SSB: voice communication over long distances.
  • CW: Morse-code communication with remarkable efficiency.
  • DXing: searching for distant and unusual stations.
  • Contesting: making many contacts in a limited period.

02 • VHF & UHF

Closer to home, radio becomes local.

VHF and UHF are where many operators first discover repeaters, simplex contacts, portable operation and local amateur-radio communities.

VHF • UHF • FM

Simplex, repeaters and local communication.

A handheld radio and a suitable antenna can be enough to start exploring local amateur radio.

  • Simplex: radio-to-radio communication.
  • Repeaters: stations that extend coverage over larger areas.
  • Portable operation: take the station outdoors.
  • Public service: participate in organized communication activities.
2 m70 cmFMRepeaters
03 • MORSE

A key, a tone and nothing more.

Morse code remains one of amateur radio's most recognizable modes. It is simple, efficient and deeply connected to radio history.

CW • CONTINUOUS WAVE

Turn characters into radio.

With CW, information is represented by short and long elements. Skilled operators can communicate with surprisingly weak signals.

  • Extremely narrow bandwidth.
  • Works well when signals are weak.
  • Can be used with simple equipment.
  • Combines operating skill with listening ability.
CWMorseQSODX
04 • DIGITAL MODES

Let the computer listen for you.

Modern digital modes allow computers to decode signals that can be extremely difficult to hear by ear.

FT8 • FT4 • WEAK SIGNAL

Signals can be almost invisible.

FT8 and related modes use carefully structured digital transmissions and computer decoding to make weak-signal communication practical.

  • FT8: popular weak-signal digital communication.
  • FT4: faster exchanges for suitable conditions.
  • RTTY / PSK: other computer-based radio modes.
  • Logging: contacts can be automatically recorded.

Digital voice systems add structured networking and data capabilities to amateur-radio communication.

🌐

DMR

Digital voice • Talk groups • Repeaters • Hotspots
🔵

D-STAR

Digital voice • Data • Gateways • Repeaters

C4FM

06 • APRS

Put information on the air.

APRS combines packet radio with position reports, messages, weather information and other useful data.

APRS • PACKET RADIO

Radio can carry data.

Instead of simply transmitting a conversation, a radio can exchange structured information with other stations and networks.

  • Position reports.
  • Short messages.
  • Weather information.
  • Digipeating and packet networks.
APRSPacketGPSMessaging
07 • HYBRID RADIO

Sometimes radio meets the internet.

Some amateur-radio systems use the internet as part of the connection between radio stations, repeaters and operators.

🔗

EchoLink

Links amateur-radio stations and operators through internet-connected systems.

🖥️

AllStar

A flexible linking system used to connect amateur-radio nodes and repeaters.

🌍

IRLP

Internet Radio Linking Project systems connect amateur-radio stations through the internet.

08 • SSTV

What if your radio could send a picture?

Slow-scan television allows images to be transmitted over radio, turning a voice channel into a surprisingly visual medium.

🖼️

Pictures

Transmit still images using audio-frequency signals carried by radio.

📻

Sound Becomes Image

A computer or decoder turns the received tones back into a picture.

🛰️

Space Images

SSTV has also been used in amateur-radio space activities.

09 • SATELLITES

Your next contact might be above you.

Amateur satellites allow operators to communicate using spacecraft passing overhead. Suddenly, the sky becomes part of your station.

SPACE COMMUNICATION

Reach space with amateur radio.

With suitable equipment, antennas and tracking information, operators can make contacts through amateur satellites and participate in ISS-related amateur-radio activities.

SatellitesISSARISSTracking
10 • PROPAGATION

The path between two radios is never quite simple.

Radio signals interact with the atmosphere, terrain, frequency, antennas and the time of day. Understanding propagation becomes an activity in itself.

The Ionosphere

Different regions of the atmosphere can refract, absorb or influence radio signals in different ways.

The Moon

With enough skill and equipment, operators can use the Moon as a reflector for Earth-Moon-Earth communication.

11 • OUTDOOR RADIO

Take the station outside.

Amateur radio does not have to stay in the shack. Portable operation combines radio with hiking, parks, mountains and exploration.

🌳

POTA

Parks on the Air encourages operators to activate parks and make contacts from outdoor locations.

⛰️

SOTA

Summits on the Air combines amateur radio with climbing and mountain exploration.

🎒

Portable

A lightweight station, battery and portable antenna can create a complete radio setup away from home.

12 • CONTESTING

How many contacts can you make in one session?

Contesting turns amateur radio into a fast-paced operating challenge where operators try to make many contacts while following the rules of a particular contest.

🏆

Speed

Efficient operating and quick exchanges are central to many contests.

📋

Logging

Accurate logging becomes essential when making large numbers of contacts.

🌍

Competition

Operators compete individually or as teams according to contest rules.

13 • DXING

Sometimes the goal is simply to hear someone far away.

DXing is the pursuit of distant or unusual contacts. It can become a long-term hobby involving propagation, antennas, operating skill and patience.

DX • DISTANT STATIONS

Distance becomes the challenge.

You may spend an entire evening waiting for a particular country, island, prefix or rare station to appear on the band.

  • Study propagation conditions.
  • Improve your antenna system.
  • Listen carefully for weak stations.
  • Build a record of contacts and confirmations.
DXPropagationAntennas
14 • RADIO DIRECTION FINDING

Can you find the hidden transmitter?

Radio direction finding and fox hunting turn radio into a physical search. Operators use signal strength, direction-finding techniques and antennas to locate a hidden transmitter.

🦊

Fox Hunting

Search for hidden low-power transmitters using radio direction-finding techniques.

📐

Direction

Use antennas and signal behaviour to determine where a transmitter is located.

🏃

Sport

Combine radio skills, navigation, physical activity and fun.

15 • PUBLIC SERVICE

When normal communication isn't available.

Amateur radio can provide independent communication capability when conventional infrastructure is damaged, overloaded or unavailable.

🚨

Emergency Nets

Operators can organize structured communication during emergencies and disasters.

🔋

Independent Power

Battery, solar and portable stations can continue operating without normal mains infrastructure.

📡

Portable Networks

VHF, UHF, HF and digital systems can create temporary communication links.

16 • BUILD IT YOURSELF

You can even build the station.

Amateur radio has a long tradition of experimentation. You can design antennas, build accessories, learn electronics and modify your station.

🔧 Homebrewing

Build equipment, interfaces and accessories instead of buying everything ready-made.

📐 Antenna Design

Experiment with dipoles, verticals, Yagis, loops, beams and other antennas.

⚡ RF Experimentation

Learn what impedance, resonance, SWR and matching actually mean.

💻 Software

Use computers for decoding, logging, satellite tracking and digital modes.

🧪 Measurement

Learn to use meters, analysers, oscilloscopes and test equipment.

🛰️ Tracking

Track satellites and study how antennas and propagation affect signals.

Which part of amateur radio will become your thing?

There is no single way to be a radio amateur. You can be a DXer, an electronics experimenter, a satellite operator, a contest operator, a digital-mode enthusiast, an outdoor operator, a Morse enthusiast — or all of them.
