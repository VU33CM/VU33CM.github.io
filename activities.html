FIRST — UNDERSTAND THE HOBBY

A licensed gateway into the world of radio

Amateur radio combines communication, electronics, science, engineering, experimentation, competition, outdoor activity, community service and pure curiosity.

Why does it need a license?

Amateur radio is not simply a set of walkie-talkies. Licensed operators are given access to designated portions of the radio spectrum so they can communicate, experiment and learn without causing harmful interference to other radio services.

Internationally, the amateur and amateur-satellite services are recognized in the ITU Radio Regulations. In India, amateur operators must obtain the required authorization and meet the applicable examination and regulatory requirements.

📜
It is a regulated radio service You operate within assigned frequencies, power limits and operating rules.
🧠
You learn before you transmit Radio theory, operating practice and regulations are part of the journey.
🔬
Experimentation is encouraged Antennas, propagation, digital communication and radio technology are all part of the hobby.
🚨
It can serve the community Amateur radio can provide useful communication support during emergencies and disasters.
📡

From electronics to outer space

The same hobby can introduce you to RF engineering, antennas, software, networking, satellites, propagation and radio science.

01 • RADIO / RF

HF — Where the world opens up

High Frequency amateur radio is where many operators discover the magic of hearing stations hundreds or thousands of kilometres away.

HF • 3–30 MHz

Voice, Morse and worldwide DX

HF radio can use the ionosphere to carry signals far beyond the horizon. Depending on frequency, time of day, solar conditions and the antenna, one station may communicate with another across a city, a country or another continent.

  • SSB voice — natural two-way conversations.
  • CW / Morse — communication using dots and dashes.
  • Digital modes — computers decode extremely weak signals.
  • DXing — searching for distant and rare stations.
  • Contesting — making many contacts against the clock.

02 • LOCAL RADIO

VHF & UHF — your local radio world

These bands are where handheld radios, mobile stations, repeaters, satellites, APRS and many local activities come alive.

VHF • UHF

The bands that connect your area

VHF and UHF are ideal for local and regional communication. A small handheld radio can become much more useful when connected to a good antenna or a repeater.

  • FM simplex conversations
  • Local amateur-radio nets
  • VHF/UHF repeaters
  • Mobile and portable operation
  • APRS and packet radio
  • Digital voice systems
  • Amateur satellite operation
03 • THE ORIGINAL DIGITAL MODE

Morse Code — radio reduced to rhythm

Before computers could decode radio signals, operators used a simple key to turn a transmitter on and off. The pattern became letters.

CW • CONTINUOUS WAVE

Dots. Dashes. And a human skill.

Morse remains popular because it can work with very simple equipment and very weak signals. A skilled operator can pull useful information out of conditions where voice communication may be difficult.

  • Excellent for weak-signal work
  • Small and simple equipment
  • Popular in DX and contests
  • A satisfying skill to learn
04 • DIGITAL RADIO

When the computer joins the radio

Modern amateur radio can combine an RF transceiver with a computer, sound interface and software. The result is a huge collection of digital modes.

📶

FT8

One of the most popular weak-signal digital modes. Computers exchange structured messages using very small slices of radio spectrum.

Famous for making contacts when signals are too weak for comfortable voice work.
⚡

FT4

A fast digital mode related to FT8, especially useful when operators want quicker exchanges.

Popular for digital contesting and weak-signal communication.
📡

RTTY / PSK31

Earlier generations of computer-based amateur data communication remain interesting for learning how radio carries text.

Great examples of turning an audio channel into a data channel.
🌐

WSPR

Designed for very weak signals and propagation experiments.

More about discovering how far your signal can travel than having a conversation.
📨

Packet Radio

Digital information sent as packets over radio links.

An important bridge between amateur radio and networking.
🖼️

SSTV

Slow Scan Television sends pictures over radio, turning a voice channel into an image link.

Especially exciting when receiving pictures from space.
FT8 / DIGITAL SIGNALS

You can actually see radio

Software such as WSJT-X displays radio activity as a waterfall. Instead of listening to every signal, the computer can detect structured digital transmissions and help decode them.

This is one of the moments where amateur radio starts feeling less like a traditional radio hobby and more like a combination of RF engineering, software and signal processing.

05 • DIGITAL VOICE

DMR, D-STAR & C4FM

Digital voice brings another dimension to VHF and UHF amateur radio. Instead of sending analogue audio directly, the radio converts speech into digital information.

📻

DMR

Digital Mobile Radio. It uses digital voice and organized talk groups to connect operators locally and across linked systems.

Often associated with repeaters, hotspots and talk groups.
🔵

D-STAR

A digital amateur-radio system designed specifically for digital voice and data.

Used with compatible radios, repeaters and gateways.
🔷

C4FM / System Fusion

A digital voice system used widely with compatible VHF/UHF amateur radios.

Can combine digital and conventional FM operation depending on the system.
06 • RADIO + DATA + LOCATION

APRS — radio that knows where it is

APRS allows amateur stations to exchange position information, short messages and other useful telemetry through packet-radio networks.

APRS • PACKET RADIO

Turn radio into a small data network

APRS can transmit location and short information using radio. Digipeaters can repeat packets, while gateways can connect parts of the system.

  • Position reporting
  • Short messages
  • Weather information
  • Tracking mobile or portable stations
  • Amateur-radio experimentation
07 • HYBRID RADIO

When radio meets the internet

Some amateur-radio systems use the internet as part of the connection. The RF link is still important, but the internet can connect distant radio systems.

ECHOLINK • ALLSTAR • IRLP

A radio conversation can cross the internet

Systems such as EchoLink, AllStar and IRLP can link amateur-radio stations and repeaters through internet-connected nodes.

For a newcomer, the important idea is simple: the radio remains part of the experience, while the internet provides a bridge between distant radio systems.

08 • SPACE RADIO

Yes — you can talk through space

Amateur radio has been used with satellites for decades, and amateur-radio equipment has been carried aboard crewed spacecraft including the International Space Station.

ARISS • AMATEUR SATELLITES

The sky is not the limit.

Amateur operators can communicate through satellites, track spacecraft, make contacts during satellite passes and participate in educational contacts through programmes such as ARISS.

The International Space Station has hosted amateur-radio operations and educational contacts, bringing amateur radio directly into the world of human spaceflight.

🛰️

Satellite Contacts

Track an amateur satellite, predict its pass and try to make a contact while it crosses your sky.

Often requires careful timing, antenna positioning and frequency control.
👨‍🚀

ISS / ARISS

Amateur radio aboard the ISS can support contacts with radio amateurs and educational programmes.

One of the most inspiring examples of amateur radio meeting space exploration.
📺

Space SSTV

Some amateur-space activities transmit images using SSTV. Receiving one can feel like catching a photograph from orbit.

A simple receiver and suitable antenna can make space reception possible.
09 • RADIO IMAGES

SSTV — sending pictures by radio

Slow Scan Television sends an image gradually through a radio channel. Instead of a voice conversation, the receiver reconstructs a picture.

SSTV

A radio can carry an image

SSTV is one of the easiest ways to understand that amateur radio is much more than speech. The radio sends tones and software turns those tones back into an image.

  • Station-to-station image exchange
  • Space and ISS image reception
  • Digital and analogue SSTV systems
  • Excellent introduction to radio + computer signal processing
10 • RADIO SCIENCE

Moonbounce — use the Moon as a reflector

Earth–Moon–Earth communication, usually called EME or Moonbounce, is one of the most technically fascinating activities in amateur radio.

Earth → Moon → Earth

A powerful directional antenna sends radio energy toward the Moon. A tiny portion of the energy is reflected back toward Earth and can be detected by another station.

  • Highly directional antennas
  • Very weak received signals
  • Precise operating techniques
  • A real radio-science experiment

Why it is difficult

The Moon is far away, the reflected signal is extremely weak and the path changes as the Moon moves.

  • Antenna gain matters
  • Receiver sensitivity matters
  • Timing and Doppler matter
  • Software can help decode weak signals
11 • RADIO PROPAGATION

Sometimes the atmosphere becomes part of the station

Radio signals do not always travel in a simple straight line. Amateur operators experiment with unusual propagation paths and learn how the atmosphere, ionosphere and weather influence radio.

☀️ Ionospheric Propagation

HF signals can be affected dramatically by solar and ionospheric conditions.

🌠 Meteor Scatter

Short-lived ionized trails from meteors can reflect radio signals.

🌤️ Tropospheric Propagation

Atmospheric conditions can extend VHF and UHF communication beyond normal expectations.

🌧️ Rain Scatter

At microwave frequencies, precipitation can become part of interesting propagation experiments.

📡 Weak Signal

Special digital techniques can recover information from remarkably weak signals.

🌍 DX Propagation

Discover why the same frequency can behave completely differently at different times.

12 • TAKE RADIO OUTSIDE

Amateur radio is also an outdoor hobby

You don't need a permanent shack. Some of the most enjoyable activities involve taking a lightweight station into parks, hills, beaches and remote locations.

🌳

POTA

Parks on the Air combines portable amateur radio with operating from parks and protected areas.

Radio + outdoors + contacts + exploration.
⛰️

SOTA

Summits on the Air combines hiking, climbing and portable amateur radio.

Reach the summit, deploy the antenna, make contacts and enjoy the view.
🚐

Mobile & Portable

Operate from a vehicle, campsite, beach, hilltop or temporary station.

A battery, radio and suitable antenna can be enough to get on the air.
13 • RADIO SPORT

Some people chase countries. Others chase records.

Amateur radio can become competitive if you want it to. Contesting, DX awards and special events turn ordinary contacts into challenges.

🏆

Contests

Make as many valid contacts as possible within a defined period.

Speed, accuracy, station setup and operating technique all matter.
🌍

DXing

Search for distant stations, rare countries and special event stations.

Propagation and patience are often as important as transmitter power.
💌

QSL Cards

Confirm contacts and exchange cards or electronic confirmations.

The contact becomes a memory you can keep.
14 • RADIO HUNTING

Find the hidden transmitter

ARDF — Amateur Radio Direction Finding — turns radio into an outdoor search game.

🦊 Fox Hunting

A hidden low-power transmitter, often called a “fox”, is placed somewhere in an area. Participants use directional antennas and signal-strength clues to locate it.

📡
Direction finding Learn where a signal is coming from.
🧭
Navigation Combine radio information with movement and terrain.
🏃
Outdoor activity A radio activity that gets you away from the desk.
🦊

Radio becomes a treasure hunt.

It is one of the clearest examples of how amateur radio can combine technology, sport and fun.

Emergency Nets

16 • BUILD IT YOURSELF

You can even build the station

Amateur radio has a long tradition of experimentation. You can design antennas, build accessories, learn electronics and modify your station.

🔧 Homebrewing

Build equipment, interfaces and accessories instead of buying everything ready-made.

📐 Antenna Design

Experiment with dipoles, verticals, Yagis, loops, beams and other antennas.

⚡ RF Experimentation

Learn what impedance, resonance, SWR and matching actually mean.

💻 Software

Use computers for decoding, logging, satellite tracking and digital modes.

🧪 Measurement

Learn to use meters, analysers, oscilloscopes and test equipment.

🛰️ Tracking

Track satellites and study how antennas and propagation affect signals.

There is no single way to be a radio amateur. You can be a DXer, an electronics experimenter, a satellite operator, a contest operator, a digital-mode enthusiast, an outdoor operator, a Morse enthusiast — or all of them.
