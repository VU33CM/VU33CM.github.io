Ham Radio Activities & Modes

Explore the many ways Indian hams communicate and enjoy the hobby.

HF – High Frequency (3–30 MHz)

Long-distance communication across India and the world. Popular bands: 80m, 40m, 20m, 15m and 10m.

  • SSB Voice – Traditional voice contacts
  • CW (Morse Code) – Excellent for weak signals and DX
  • Digital modes – FT8, JT65, PSK31, RTTY

Best for DXing, contests and making friends worldwide.

VHF / UHF

Local and regional communication. Main bands in India: 2 metres (144–146 MHz) and 70 cm (430–440 MHz).

  • FM voice (simplex and via repeaters)
  • Digital voice: DMR, C4FM, D-STAR
  • APRS for position tracking and messaging
  • Packet radio

Ideal for local nets, mobile operation and emergency communication.

FT8 – Popular Digital Mode

Extremely popular weak-signal digital mode. Works even with low power and simple antennas.

  • Uses free WSJT-X software
  • Very active on 20m, 40m and 15m
  • Great for beginners to make worldwide contacts
  • Short, structured transmissions – perfect for poor conditions

DMR – Digital Mobile Radio

Digital voice mode mainly used on VHF/UHF.

  • Clear audio and efficient spectrum use
  • Talk groups for national and international contacts
  • Requires a DMR radio and free DMR ID (from radioid.net)
  • Many Indian cities have DMR repeaters or personal hotspots

EchoLink

Connects your radio or computer to the internet so you can talk to other hams worldwide.

  • Useful when you have limited antenna space or are travelling
  • Many Indian repeaters and individual stations are linked
  • Works with smartphone apps, computer or radio nodes

Importance of Repeaters

Repeaters are the backbone of local VHF/UHF activity in India.

  • Extend the range of handheld and mobile radios from a few km to 50–100+ km
  • Enable group nets and emergency communication in cities and hills
  • Many are linked via EchoLink or AllStar for wider reach
  • Most Indian VHF repeaters use –600 kHz shift

QSL Cards

A QSL card is a confirmation of a radio contact (QSO). It is part of the rich culture of amateur radio.

Why QSL cards matter

  • Confirm that a contact really took place
  • Required for many awards (DXCC, WAS, IOTA, etc.)
  • Serve as personal souvenirs and collectibles
  • Build friendships between operators

Modern QSLing methods

  • Physical paper cards (via bureau or direct mail)
  • Electronic: eQSL.cc and Logbook of The World (LoTW)
  • QRZ.com and Club Log also support confirmations

Many Indian hams still enjoy receiving a nice paper QSL card with your callsign and a photo of your station or local area.

Other Popular Activities

  • Contests – Compete to make maximum contacts in a limited time
  • POTA / SOTA – Parks on the Air & Summits on the Air
  • Satellite Communication – Talk through amateur satellites and the ISS
  • Fox Hunting / ARDF – Radio direction finding (fun outdoor activity)
  • Emergency Communication (EmComm) – Support during disasters
  • Homebrewing – Building your own radios, antennas and accessories
